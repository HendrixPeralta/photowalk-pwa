# PhotoWalk — 5-Minute Presentation

*Audience: high school and university students. Pace ≈ 150 words/min, ~790 words. Timings are cumulative. Stage directions in italics.*

---

## 0:00 — Hook (40 sec)

*Open with a show of hands. Wait for it — let the room actually answer.*

Quick question. Hands up if you have more than a thousand photos on your phone.

*Pause.*

Keep your hand up if you'd call more than ten of them *good*.

*Pause. Most hands drop.*

Right. That's the gap I care about. It's not that you don't take photos — you take hundreds. It's that taking hundreds of photos doesn't automatically make you better at taking them. You can shoot for three years and plateau in the first three months.

So why does that happen? Two reasons.

## 0:40 — The two problems (35 sec)

**One: you can't see what you're looking at.** You scroll past a photo that stops you dead, and you think "that's incredible" — and then you can't say *why*. Not one specific reason. And if you can't name it, you can't steal it, and if you can't steal it, you'll never do it on purpose.

**Two: you don't practice.** Not because you're lazy — because practice is invisible. You never *see* yourself improving, so there's nothing pulling you back out the door.

PhotoWalk is an app that attacks both. Let me show you.

## 1:15 — Part 1: See what you're looking at (105 sec)

*Switch to the Analyze screen. Load a photo the audience can react to.*

Here's a photo. It looks good. Why?

Let's find out — manually. Nothing here is AI. No model is going to score this photo an 8 out of 10, because a score teaches you *nothing*. The whole point is that **you** learn to see it.

*Drag a thirds overlay onto the image.*

First, composition guides you can actually move. Rule of thirds, golden ratio, golden spiral — draggable, rotatable, zoomable. Not a fixed grid slapped on top. You line it up against the real subject and watch it click into place. That's the moment where "it just looks nice" turns into "the horizon is on the lower third and her eye is on the intersection."

*Switch to the scopes view.*

Now light and color. This is a histogram and these are scopes — the same tools colorists use on films you've watched. They're honest in a way your eyes aren't, because your eyes adapt and lie to you.

But here's the thing: scopes are useless if nobody ever taught you to read one. So look down here.

*Point at the caption line.*

The app writes it out in plain English. *"Your shadows are blue." "Your highlights are blown out — that detail is gone forever." "This palette is complementary — orange and teal."*

That's the part that matters. It's not grading you. It's giving you **vocabulary**. And once you have the words, you start seeing it everywhere — in films, in ads, in your own camera roll.

*Open compare mode.*

And you can put your shot next to a photo you admire, side by side, same tools on both. That comparison is the fastest teacher in the app.

## 3:00 — Part 2: Make practice visible (95 sec)

*Switch to Home.*

Okay, second problem. Knowing things doesn't make you good. Reps do.

*Point at the heatmap.*

This is a year of your photography. Every square is a day, and the darker it is, the more you shot. It's the GitHub contribution grid, or your Duolingo streak, pointed at a creative skill instead of a language.

And it works for the same annoying, undignified reason those work: **you don't want to break the chain.** You look at that and you feel it. That's not a bug — that's the feature.

*Point at the rewards section.*

But streaks alone burn out, so there's one more piece, and it's my favorite. You set your own rewards. You name something you actually want — new lens, concert tickets, a very expensive coffee — and you price it in *hours of shooting*.

Here's the important part: it snapshots your hours the moment you set it. Nothing you did before counts. You start at zero, every time.

So the reward isn't handed to you by the app. It's one you chose, that you paid for in practice hours. And the app just quietly keeps the receipt.

*Point at the walk themes, briefly.*

And when you don't know what to shoot, it hands you a theme — shadows and silhouettes, leading lines, night lights — picked from whatever you've practiced *least*, and adjusted for what time of day it actually is. No blank page. Just go outside.

## 4:35 — Close (25 sec)

So that's PhotoWalk. Two ideas.

**Make looking teachable** — turn "that photo is nice" into words you can use.

And **make practice visible** — so the hours you put in show up somewhere you can see them.

Your phone is already a good enough camera. This is about the part behind it.

Thank you.

---

## Delivery notes

- **The hands-up opener is the whole hook.** Don't rush it — the second question is what lands.
- **Have one photo pre-loaded** in Analyze. Fumbling with a file picker on stage kills 20 seconds.
- Pick a demo photo with **obvious blue shadows or clipped highlights** so the plain-language caption says something dramatic.
- If you're running long, cut compare mode (0:15) and the walk-themes aside (0:20).
- If asked "why no AI?": *because an AI score tells you what it thinks, not how to see. The goal is your eye, not the app's.*
